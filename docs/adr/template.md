## Context

What situation forced a decision? What constraints applied — budget, time,
existing systems, team size? What would happen if nothing were decided?

State facts, not conclusions. The reader should be able to reach a different
decision from the same context and understand why.

## Decision

What was chosen, stated plainly. Include the specific configuration or mechanism
where it matters.

### Alternatives considered

What else was on the table, and why it lost. A decision with no rejected
alternatives usually means the problem wasn't examined.

## Implementation notes

Anything non-obvious discovered while building it — ordering constraints,
surprising defaults, failure modes. This section is often the most useful part
of the record six months later.

## Consequences

**Positive** — what this buys.

**Negative** — what it costs, in money, complexity, or flexibility.

**Accepted risks** — what could go wrong that is being tolerated, and under what
conditions the decision should be revisited.
